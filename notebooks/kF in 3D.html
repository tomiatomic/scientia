Density of states in kkk-space

refers to how many quantum states are available per unit volume in momentum space (or wavevector space). This concept is crucial for counting the number of electron states up to the Fermi level.

Why do we need it?

Electrons in a box have allowed wavevectors given by:

kx,ky,kz=2πLnx,ny,nzk_x, k_y, k_z = \frac{2\pi}{L} n_x, n_y, n_z kx​,ky​,kz​=L2π​nx​,ny​,nz​

So the allowed kkk-points form a grid in 3D kkk-space with spacing:

Δk=2πL\Delta k = \frac{2\pi}{L} Δk=L2π​

Fermi wavevector

In the free electron model, kFk_FkF​ refers to the Fermi wavevector, which is the magnitude of the wavevector corresponding to the highest occupied energy state at absolute zero temperature.
